# Problem Statement

Physical security operations today are fundamentally reactive. Guards monitor live feeds, respond to incidents after they occur, and rely on manual review of recorded footage to reconstruct what happened. At scale — across multiple sites, dozens of cameras, and thousands of daily movements — this model breaks down. Attention degrades, incidents are missed in real time, and by the time a threat is identified, the window to respond has often already closed.

Existing surveillance infrastructure compounds the problem rather than solving it. Most facilities already have cameras, but those cameras are passive — they record everything and understand nothing. The footage they generate is only as useful as the human capacity to watch it, which is finite, expensive, and error-prone.

The gap is not hardware. The gap is intelligence.

Innovision Limited operates across corporate campuses, government facilities, industrial sites, and infrastructure deployments — environments where the cost of a missed incident, an unauthorized entry, or an undetected crowd risk is significant. Managing this at scale with human monitoring alone is neither reliable nor sustainable.

What is needed is a system that converts existing passive camera infrastructure into an active, real-time security intelligence layer — one that recognizes and tracks individuals across cameras, detects unauthorized presence automatically, monitors crowd density before it becomes a crisis, enforces virtual zone boundaries without physical barriers, and delivers actionable alerts to operators in the moment rather than after the fact.

Critically, such a system must handle biometric data with the rigor that India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Innovision's existing ISO 27001 obligations demand. A facial recognition system that creates compliance liability is not a security asset — it is a risk in itself.

We are building that system: a multi-analytics computer vision platform that processes live camera feeds in real time, delivers eight integrated security intelligence capabilities — face recognition with deduplication, face tokenization, restricted entry detection, headcount monitoring, crowd density management, zone-based monitoring, intruder detection, and pedestrian detection — and does so within a privacy-by-design architecture that treats regulatory compliance as a first-class requirement, not an afterthought.
